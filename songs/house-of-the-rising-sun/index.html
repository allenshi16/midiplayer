About House of the Rising Sun

House of the Rising Sun is one of the most beloved public-domain melodies available to play online. House of the Rising Sun is a traditional American folk song, with the earliest known version dating to around 1905. The song tells of a life gone wrong in New Orleans, with the "House of the Rising Sun" variously interpreted as a prison, a brothel, or a gambling house. The most famous recording is by The Animals (1964), whose electric arrangement transformed the folk ballad into a rock classic. The traditional melody is in the Dorian mode, giving it a haunting, ambiguous tonality.

As a public-domain composition, House of the Rising Sun can be freely performed, arranged, and studied by musicians of all levels. The melody has endured for generations because of its memorable contour and emotional resonance.

Musical structure and analysis

This arrangement is written in A minor at 100 BPM. The traditional melody is in A minor (Dorian mode) with a slow, ballad-like character. The form is a 12-bar blues-like structure with three four-bar phrases. The melody uses the raised 6th (F♯) characteristic of Dorian mode, creating the distinctive melancholy-but-not-quite-minor sound. The arpeggiated guitar pattern (in the Animals version) follows an Am–C–D–F progression.

Difficulty level and practice tips

Beginner to intermediate. The melody is slow and mostly stepwise. The Dorian mode means some intervals may feel unfamiliar. The main challenge is capturing the emotional weight of the piece — it should sound mournful and reflective.

Focus on the Dorian raised 6th — it is the characteristic note that gives the song its unique sound. Practice the melody slowly with a singing tone. The arpeggiated accompaniment pattern (if playing guitar-style) should be steady and hypnotic. Use our MIDI player to study the modal intervals.

Did you know?

The Animals' 1964 recording of House of the Rising Sun was done in a single take. The famous organ intro was an accident — organist Alan Price was warming up when the recording started, and the producer liked the sound, so they kept it. The song reached #1 on both the UK and US charts.

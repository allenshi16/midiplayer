About Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy

Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy is one of the most beloved public-domain melodies available to play online. Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy is from Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky's ballet The Nutcracker, composed in 1892. The dance represents the Sugar Plum Fairy welcoming Clara to the Land of Sweets in Act II. Tchaikovsky famously used the celesta — a newly invented keyboard instrument that produces a bell-like sound — for this piece, keeping its use secret from rival composers. The celesta's ethereal, tinkling sound perfectly captures the magical, delicate character of the Sugar Plum Fairy.

As a public-domain composition, Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy can be freely performed, arranged, and studied by musicians of all levels. The melody has endured for generations because of its memorable contour and emotional resonance.

Musical structure and analysis

This arrangement is written in E minor at 130 BPM. The piece is in E minor with a delicate, staccato character. The celesta plays a light, dancing melody over a pizzicato string accompaniment. The form is a simple ternary (A–B–A) with the celesta theme returning after a brief contrasting section. The 6/8 meter gives it a dancing, triple-time feel.

Difficulty level and practice tips

Intermediate. The staccato notes require precise, light articulation. The E minor key uses one sharp (F♯). The main challenge is the delicate touch — the notes should be crisp but not heavy. The fast tempo requires good finger control.

The staccato articulation is essential — each note should be short and light, like sugar plums dancing. Practice the melody with a very light touch. The pizzicato accompaniment (if playing both hands) should be even lighter than the melody. Use our MIDI player to slow the tempo and study the articulation.

Did you know?

Tchaikovsky discovered the celesta in Paris in 1891 and was so enchanted by its sound that he bought one secretly, fearing that Rimsky-Korsakov or Glazunov might use the instrument before him. The Sugar Plum Fairy dance was the celesta's debut in Russian orchestral music.

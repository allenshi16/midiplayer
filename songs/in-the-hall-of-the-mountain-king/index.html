About In the Hall of the Mountain King

In the Hall of the Mountain King is one of the most beloved public-domain melodies available to play online. In the Hall of the Mountain King is from Edvard Grieg's incidental music for Henrik Ibsen's play Peer Gynt, composed in 1875. The piece depicts the character Peer Gynt being chased by trolls in the mountain hall of the Dovre King. The music starts slowly and quietly, then accelerates and grows louder as the chase intensifies — a masterful example of musical storytelling through tempo and dynamics. It has become one of the most recognizable pieces of classical music.

As a public-domain composition, In the Hall of the Mountain King can be freely performed, arranged, and studied by musicians of all levels. The melody has endured for generations because of its memorable contour and emotional resonance.

Musical structure and analysis

This arrangement is written in E minor at 140 BPM. The piece is in E minor with a relentless, building character. It is built on a single, repeating theme that starts slowly in a low register and gradually accelerates, rises in pitch, and grows louder. The form is a continuous crescendo and accelerando — there is no traditional structure, just an unbroken build from whisper to frenzy. The 4/4 meter provides a steady pulse that the tempo deviations play against.

Difficulty level and practice tips

Intermediate to advanced. The piece starts easy but becomes increasingly demanding as the tempo accelerates. The E minor key uses one sharp (F♯). The main challenge is the accelerating tempo — the player must maintain clarity and control as the speed increases dramatically.

Start the piece at the slow, quiet opening and gradually build speed and volume. The theme should be played with increasing urgency. Practice the accelerando separately — plan where you will increase the tempo. The final section should be wild and frenzied. Use our MIDI player to study how the tempo changes throughout.

Did you know?

In Ibsen's play, the trolls sing "Slå dem! Troll-kokken! Stor-kokken!" (Beat him! Troll-cook! Big-cook!) as they chase Peer Gynt. The music's accelerando represents Peer's increasing panic as the trolls close in. Grieg originally called the piece "Trolltog" (Troll March).

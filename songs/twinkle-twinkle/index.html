About Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star

Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star is one of the most beloved public-domain melodies available to play online. Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star is one of the most universally known nursery rhymes in the English-speaking world. The lyrics come from a poem by Jane Taylor published in 1806 titled "The Star." The melody is a French tune called "Ah! vous dirai-je, maman" which dates to 1761 and was later used by Mozart for his famous variations (K. 265). The combination of Taylor's words and the French melody became the standard English version by the mid-19th century.

As a public-domain composition, Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star can be freely performed, arranged, and studied by musicians of all levels. The melody has endured for generations because of its memorable contour and emotional resonance.

Musical structure and analysis

This arrangement is written in C major at 100 BPM. The melody is in simple binary form (A–B). The A section presents the ascending theme (C–C–G–G–A–A–G) and the B section offers a gentle contrast (F–F–E–E–D–D–C) before resolving back to the tonic. The entire melody spans just one octave (C4 to C5), making it ideal for beginner instruments.

Difficulty level and practice tips

Absolute beginner. All notes fall within the C major scale with no sharps or flats. The rhythm uses only quarter notes and half notes. This is typically one of the first melodies taught to piano, violin, and recorder students.

This is the perfect piece for learning to read music. Match each note on the piano roll to its position on your keyboard. Practice saying the note names as you play them. Once comfortable with the melody, try playing it in different octaves or adding a simple bass line (C and G alternating).

Did you know?

Mozart composed 12 variations on this melody (K. 265) around 1778, making it one of the earliest examples of a popular tune being used as the basis for classical composition. The same French melody is also used for the alphabet song ("A-B-C-D-E-F-G").
